# Glossary

The terms Neural Networks from Scratch defines, in alphabetical order, each with the post that introduces it. Symbols follow the [notation guide](notation_guide.md): bold capitals for batch matrices ($\mathbf{X}$, $\mathbf{W}$, $\mathbf{Z}$), bold lowercase for vectors ($\mathbf{b}$, $\hat{\mathbf{y}}$), italic for scalars.

- **Accuracy**: the fraction of samples whose predicted class, the `argmax` of the output row, equals the true class. A readable score that, unlike the loss, ignores how confident each prediction was; random guessing over $K$ classes scores about $1/K$ ([Post 08](posts/08-loss-categorical-cross-entropy/index.md)).
- **Activation function**: a function applied element-wise (ReLU, sigmoid) or row-wise (softmax) to a layer's pre-activations $\mathbf{Z}$. Without a non-linear activation between them, any stack of dense layers collapses into a single linear map ([Post 06](posts/06-activation-functions-relu-and-softmax/index.md)).
- **AdaGrad**: an optimiser that divides each parameter's step by the square root of that parameter's running sum of squared gradients, $\theta \leftarrow \theta - \alpha g / (\sqrt{G} + \epsilon)$, so parameters with a large gradient history take smaller steps. Because $G$ only grows, the effective learning rate decays towards zero over a long run ([Post 25](posts/25-adagrad/index.md)).
- **Adam**: the optimiser that combines a momentum-style moving average of gradients (first moment, $\beta_1$) with an RMSProp-style moving average of squared gradients (second moment, $\beta_2$) and corrects both for their zero start ([Post 27](posts/27-adam-optimiser/index.md)).
- **Axis**: one dimension of an array, numbered from 0. In a batch matrix axis 0 runs down the rows, one per sample, and axis 1 runs across the columns. A reduction removes the axis it names ([Post 05](posts/05-array-summation-keepdims-and-broadcasting/index.md)).
- **Backpropagation**: computing the gradient of the loss with respect to every parameter by applying the chain rule layer by layer from the loss back to the input. Each component receives the gradient of its output (`dvalues`) and returns the gradient of its input (`dinputs`) ([Post 12](posts/12-backprop-through-a-single-neuron/index.md) to [Post 21](posts/21-coding-the-full-backpropagation/index.md)).
- **Batch**: a set of samples processed together as one matrix of shape $(N, n_\text{inputs})$, one sample per row. A batch turns many dot products into one matrix product ([Post 01](posts/01-neurons-and-layers/index.md), [Post 02](posts/02-numpy-and-the-dot-product/index.md)).
- **Bias**: the constant a neuron adds to its weighted sum, one per neuron. It lets the output be non-zero when every input is zero. From Post 04 on a layer stores its biases as a $(1, n_\text{neurons})$ row that broadcasts over the batch; Posts 01 to 03 keep them in a flat list of $n_\text{neurons}$ numbers, which broadcasts the same way ([Post 01](posts/01-neurons-and-layers/index.md), [Post 04](posts/04-dense-layer-class-and-spiral-data/index.md)).
- **Bias correction**: in Adam, dividing the first and second moment estimates by $1 - \beta_1^t$ and $1 - \beta_2^t$, where $t$ counts updates from 1. Both estimates start at zero and are biased towards zero in early steps; the correction removes that bias, which matters most in the early updates ([Post 27](posts/27-adam-optimiser/index.md)).
- **Binary cross-entropy**: the loss for a yes-or-no output $\hat{y} = \sigma(z)$: $L = -[y \log \hat{y} + (1 - y) \log(1 - \hat{y})]$, averaged over the batch. Paired with the sigmoid, its gradient with respect to $z$ simplifies to $(\hat{y} - y)/N$ ([Post 34](posts/34-sigmoid-and-binary-cross-entropy/index.md)).
- **Broadcasting**: NumPy's rule for combining arrays of different shapes element-wise: shapes are compared from the last axis, and two sizes are compatible when they are equal or one of them is 1. It is what adds a $(1, n)$ bias row to every row of an $(N, n)$ output ([Post 05](posts/05-array-summation-keepdims-and-broadcasting/index.md)).
- **Cache (optimiser)**: the per-parameter store of squared gradients that AdaGrad sums and RMSProp and Adam average (`weight_cache`, `bias_cache`). A larger cache means a smaller effective step for that parameter ([Post 25](posts/25-adagrad/index.md)).
- **Categorical cross-entropy**: the loss for multi-class classification. For one sample it is $-\log \hat{y}_c$, the negative log of the probability given to the true class $c$; the reported loss is the mean over the batch. A uniform guess over three classes gives $\ln 3 \approx 1.099$ ([Post 08](posts/08-loss-categorical-cross-entropy/index.md)).
- **Chain rule**: the derivative of a composition is the product of the local derivatives along the chain, $\frac{dL}{dx} = \frac{dL}{dy} \cdot \frac{dy}{dx}$. Backpropagation is the chain rule applied in an order that reuses every intermediate product ([Post 11](posts/11-the-chain-rule/index.md)).
- **Clipping**: limiting predicted probabilities to `[1e-7, 1 - 1e-7]` before taking a logarithm, so that a zero probability cannot produce an infinite loss ([Post 08](posts/08-loss-categorical-cross-entropy/index.md)).
- **Co-adaptation**: neurons that only work in combination with particular other neurons rather than learning useful features on their own. Dropout breaks it by removing a random subset of neurons on every training pass ([Post 31](posts/31-dropout/index.md)).
- **Combined softmax and cross-entropy gradient**: the gradient of the mean categorical cross-entropy with respect to the logits when softmax and the loss are differentiated together: $(\hat{\mathbf{y}} - \mathbf{y})/N$ with $\mathbf{y}$ one-hot. It avoids building the softmax Jacobian and is the reason the series uses one combined class, `Activation_Softmax_Loss_CategoricalCrossentropy` ([Post 19](posts/19-softmax-derivatives-and-the-combined-backward-pass/index.md)).
- **Data leakage**: information from the validation or test data influencing training or model choice, for example standardising features with statistics computed on the whole dataset. It makes the reported score optimistic ([Post 29](posts/29-validation-and-hyperparameter-tuning/index.md)).
- **Dead neuron**: a ReLU neuron whose input is negative for every sample, so its output and its gradient are always zero and its weights stop updating ([Post 06](posts/06-activation-functions-relu-and-softmax/index.md)).
- **Dense layer**: a layer in which every input connects to every neuron, computing $\mathbf{Z} = \mathbf{X}\mathbf{W} + \mathbf{b}$; the series' class is `Layer_Dense` ([Post 04](posts/04-dense-layer-class-and-spiral-data/index.md)).
- **Derivative**: the rate of change of a function at a point, the slope of its tangent; for $f(x) = x^2$, $f'(x) = 2x$. Its sign says which way to move an input to increase the function ([Post 10](posts/10-derivatives-partial-derivatives-and-gradients/index.md)).
- **`dvalues`, `dweights`, `dbiases`, `dinputs`**: the code names of gradients. `dvalues` is the gradient arriving at a component from the one after it; `dweights` and `dbiases` are the parameter gradients it stores for the optimiser; `dinputs` is the gradient it passes back, which becomes the previous component's `dvalues` ([Post 15](posts/15-gradients-with-respect-to-inputs/index.md), [Post 16](posts/16-coding-backpropagation/index.md)).
- **Dot product**: the sum of the element-wise products of two vectors, $\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = \sum_i a_i b_i$. One neuron's weighted sum is a dot product; `np.dot` computes it, and on matrices it computes a matrix product ([Post 02](posts/02-numpy-and-the-dot-product/index.md)).
- **Dropout**: a regulariser that, during training only, sets each activation to zero with probability $p$ (the rate) and scales the survivors by $1/(1 - p)$, so the expected activation is unchanged and evaluation needs no adjustment (inverted dropout). `Layer_Dropout(rate)` takes the drop rate and stores the keep rate $1 - p$ ([Post 31](posts/31-dropout/index.md)).
- **Epoch**: one pass over the whole training set. With full-batch training an epoch is one update; with mini-batches of size $B$ it is $\lceil N/B \rceil$ updates ([Post 22](posts/22-gradient-descent-optimiser/index.md), [Post 32](posts/32-mini-batching/index.md)).
- **Epsilon ($\epsilon$)**: a small constant, `1e-7` in the series' optimisers, added to a denominator so it can never be zero ([Post 25](posts/25-adagrad/index.md)).
- **Feature**: one input dimension, one column of $\mathbf{X}$. Spiral data of shape $(300, 2)$ has two features ([Post 04](posts/04-dense-layer-class-and-spiral-data/index.md)).
- **Finite difference**: an estimate of a derivative from two function values a small step $h$ apart. The forward difference $(f(x + h) - f(x))/h$ has a truncation error of order $h$; the central difference $(f(x + h) - f(x - h))/(2h)$ has one of order $h^2$. In float64 the central difference is most accurate near $h = 10^{-5}$; below that, rounding error grows faster than truncation error falls ([Post 10](posts/10-derivatives-partial-derivatives-and-gradients/index.md)).
- **Forward pass**: computing the network's output from its input layer by layer, a dense step $\mathbf{Z} = \mathbf{X}\mathbf{W} + \mathbf{b}$ followed by an activation $\mathbf{A} = f(\mathbf{Z})$, ending in the loss ([Post 03](posts/03-stacking-layers-and-the-forward-pass/index.md), [Post 07](posts/07-coding-the-complete-forward-pass/index.md)).
- **Generalisation**: how well a trained model performs on data it was not trained on. The generalisation gap is the difference between training and test performance ([Post 28](posts/28-generalization-and-testing/index.md)).
- **Glorot (Xavier) initialisation**: drawing weights with variance $2/(n_\text{in} + n_\text{out})$, which keeps the variance of activations and of gradients roughly constant across layers for activations that are near-linear around zero, such as tanh and sigmoid ([Post 33](posts/33-weight-initialisation/index.md)).
- **Gradient**: the vector of partial derivatives of a function with respect to all its inputs. It points in the direction of steepest increase, so gradient descent steps the other way ([Post 10](posts/10-derivatives-partial-derivatives-and-gradients/index.md)).
- **Gradient check**: comparing an analytic gradient with a numerical one computed by central finite differences, $\frac{L(\theta + h) - L(\theta - h)}{2h}$, whose truncation error shrinks as $h^2$. In float64 and for gradients that are not themselves tiny, a relative error near $10^{-7}$ or below means the backward pass is right; above about $10^{-3}$ it is almost certainly wrong ([Post 10](posts/10-derivatives-partial-derivatives-and-gradients/index.md), [Post 21](posts/21-coding-the-full-backpropagation/index.md)).
- **Gradient descent**: updating every parameter by $\theta \leftarrow \theta - \alpha \, \partial L / \partial \theta$ and repeating; the series' `Optimizer_SGD` ([Post 22](posts/22-gradient-descent-optimiser/index.md)).
- **He initialisation**: drawing weights with variance $2/n_\text{in}$, that is `np.random.randn(n_inputs, n_neurons) * np.sqrt(2 / n_inputs)`. The factor 2 compensates for ReLU zeroing half of its inputs; it is the right default for ReLU networks ([Post 33](posts/33-weight-initialisation/index.md)).
- **Hidden layer**: any layer between the input and the output layer; its values are internal to the network and never compared with a target ([Post 03](posts/03-stacking-layers-and-the-forward-pass/index.md)).
- **Hyperparameter**: a setting chosen before training rather than learned, such as the learning rate, decay, layer sizes, regularisation strength, or dropout rate. It is chosen on validation data, never on test data ([Post 29](posts/29-validation-and-hyperparameter-tuning/index.md)).
- **Initialisation**: how weights and biases are set before training. The series starts with `0.01 * np.random.randn(n_inputs, n_neurons)` and zero biases; the random weights make the neurons differ from each other. Post 33 replaces the fixed scale with Glorot or He scaling ([Post 04](posts/04-dense-layer-class-and-spiral-data/index.md), [Post 33](posts/33-weight-initialisation/index.md)).
- **Jacobian**: the matrix of partial derivatives of every output of a vector function with respect to every input. It is diagonal for an element-wise function such as ReLU and full for a coupled one such as softmax ([Post 17](posts/17-backpropagation-through-activation-functions/index.md)).
- **k-fold cross-validation**: splitting the training data into $k$ folds, training $k$ times with a different fold held out for validation each time, and averaging the $k$ scores; used when one validation split would be too small to trust ([Post 29](posts/29-validation-and-hyperparameter-tuning/index.md)).
- **`keepdims`**: the NumPy argument that keeps a reduced axis with size 1. `np.sum(a, axis=1, keepdims=True)` on a $(3, 4)$ array returns $(3, 1)$, a column that broadcasts back across the rows, instead of $(3,)$ ([Post 05](posts/05-array-summation-keepdims-and-broadcasting/index.md)).
- **L1 regularisation**: adding $\lambda \sum |w|$ to the loss; its gradient $\lambda \, \text{sign}(w)$ has the same size for every non-zero weight, which pushes small weights to exactly zero ([Post 30](posts/30-l1-and-l2-regularisation/index.md)).
- **L2 regularisation**: adding $\lambda \sum w^2$ to the loss; its gradient $2\lambda w$ is proportional to the weight, which shrinks large weights most and rarely makes one exactly zero ([Post 30](posts/30-l1-and-l2-regularisation/index.md)).
- **Layer**: a group of neurons that receive the same inputs. In the class-based code from Post 04 its weights have shape $(n_\text{inputs}, n_\text{neurons})$, so a layer of 3 neurons over 4 inputs has a $(4, 3)$ weight matrix; Posts 01 to 03 store the transpose, one row per neuron ([Post 01](posts/01-neurons-and-layers/index.md), [Post 04](posts/04-dense-layer-class-and-spiral-data/index.md)).
- **Learning rate ($\alpha$)**: the multiplier on the gradient in each update. Too large and the loss oscillates or diverges; too small and training crawls ([Post 22](posts/22-gradient-descent-optimiser/index.md)).
- **Learning-rate decay**: shrinking the learning rate as training proceeds; the series uses $\alpha_t = \alpha_0 / (1 + d \cdot t)$ with $t$ the number of updates so far ([Post 23](posts/23-learning-rate-decay/index.md)).
- **Logits**: the raw scores of the last dense layer, before softmax turns them into probabilities ([Post 06](posts/06-activation-functions-relu-and-softmax/index.md)).
- **Loss**: one number that measures how wrong the predictions are, lower being better; the quantity the optimiser minimises ([Post 08](posts/08-loss-categorical-cross-entropy/index.md)).
- **Matrix product**: the product of an $(m, n)$ and an $(n, p)$ matrix, an $(m, p)$ matrix whose entry $(i, j)$ is the dot product of row $i$ of the first with column $j$ of the second. The inner sizes must match ([Post 02](posts/02-numpy-and-the-dot-product/index.md)).
- **Mini-batch**: a random subset of the training set, typically a power of 2 from 32 to 512 samples, used for one update. Training loops over shuffled mini-batches inside each epoch, trading a noisier gradient for many more updates per epoch ([Post 32](posts/32-mini-batching/index.md)).
- **Momentum**: an optimiser extension that keeps a velocity per parameter, $v \leftarrow \beta v - \alpha \, \partial L/\partial \theta$, and steps by it, $\theta \leftarrow \theta + v$. Components that change sign from step to step cancel and consistent ones build up, which damps zig-zagging across a narrow valley ([Post 24](posts/24-momentum/index.md)).
- **Neuron**: the basic unit: it multiplies each input by a weight, sums the products, and adds a bias, $z = \sum_i w_i x_i + b$; an activation may follow ([Post 01](posts/01-neurons-and-layers/index.md)).
- **Non-linearity**: a function that is not of the form $ax + b$. Placed between layers, it is what lets a network represent curved decision boundaries such as the spiral ([Post 06](posts/06-activation-functions-relu-and-softmax/index.md)).
- **One-hot encoding**: writing a class label as a vector with 1 at the class index and 0 elsewhere; class 2 of 3 is `[0, 0, 1]` ([Post 08](posts/08-loss-categorical-cross-entropy/index.md)).
- **Optimiser**: the object that updates the parameters from their gradients. The series builds them in order (gradient descent, decay, momentum, AdaGrad, RMSProp, Adam), all sharing the `pre_update_params`, `update_params`, `post_update_params` contract ([Post 22](posts/22-gradient-descent-optimiser/index.md) to [Post 27](posts/27-adam-optimiser/index.md)).
- **Overfitting**: a model that fits its training data much better than new data, seen as a large gap between training and test accuracy ([Post 28](posts/28-generalization-and-testing/index.md)).
- **Partial derivative**: the derivative of a function of several variables with respect to one of them, the others held constant; $\partial f / \partial w_1$ says how $f$ changes when only $w_1$ moves ([Post 10](posts/10-derivatives-partial-derivatives-and-gradients/index.md)).
- **Reduction**: an operation such as `np.sum`, `np.max`, or `np.mean` that collapses an array along one or more axes. The named axis disappears from the shape unless `keepdims=True` keeps it with size 1 ([Post 05](posts/05-array-summation-keepdims-and-broadcasting/index.md)).
- **ReLU**: the rectified linear unit, $\max(0, z)$, the series' hidden-layer activation. It passes positive inputs unchanged and outputs zero for the rest; its derivative is 1 for $z > 0$ and 0 otherwise ([Post 06](posts/06-activation-functions-relu-and-softmax/index.md)).
- **Regularisation**: any technique that reduces overfitting by constraining the model; the series covers L1 and L2 penalties and dropout ([Post 30](posts/30-l1-and-l2-regularisation/index.md), [Post 31](posts/31-dropout/index.md)).
- **RMSProp**: an optimiser that replaces AdaGrad's ever-growing sum with an exponential moving average of squared gradients, $G \leftarrow \rho G + (1 - \rho) g^2$, so the effective learning rate no longer decays to zero ([Post 26](posts/26-rmsprop/index.md)).
- **Rounding error**: the error a computed value carries because a float keeps a fixed number of digits. A float64 number has about 16 significant decimal digits, and its machine epsilon, the relative gap between neighbouring numbers, is $2.2 \times 10^{-16}$. A finite difference subtracts two nearly equal values, so its rounding error grows as the step $h$ shrinks ([Post 10](posts/10-derivatives-partial-derivatives-and-gradients/index.md)).
- **Sample**: one example, one row of $\mathbf{X}$; spiral data of shape $(300, 2)$ has 300 samples ([Post 04](posts/04-dense-layer-class-and-spiral-data/index.md)).
- **Sigmoid**: $\sigma(z) = 1/(1 + e^{-z})$, which maps any real number into $(0, 1)$; the output activation for a binary problem, read as the probability of the positive class ([Post 34](posts/34-sigmoid-and-binary-cross-entropy/index.md)).
- **Softmax**: the output activation for multi-class classification, $\hat{y}_k = e^{z_k} / \sum_j e^{z_j}$ over each row of logits, giving positive values that sum to 1. Subtracting the row maximum first leaves the result unchanged and prevents overflow ([Post 06](posts/06-activation-functions-relu-and-softmax/index.md)).
- **Spiral data**: the series' training set from Post 04 to Post 31: three interleaved spiral arms of 100 points each in two dimensions, generated by `spiral_data` from the `nnfs` package. No straight line separates the classes ([Post 04](posts/04-dense-layer-class-and-spiral-data/index.md)).
- **Test set**: data held back from training and model selection, used once at the end to report performance ([Post 28](posts/28-generalization-and-testing/index.md)).
- **Training loop**: the repeated cycle of forward pass, loss, backward pass, and optimiser update ([Post 21](posts/21-coding-the-full-backpropagation/index.md), [Post 22](posts/22-gradient-descent-optimiser/index.md)).
- **Transpose**: swapping a matrix's rows and columns, so an $(m, n)$ matrix becomes $(n, m)$; written $\mathbf{W}^\top$, or `W.T` in NumPy. In the backward pass it is what makes the shapes of the gradient products line up ([Post 02](posts/02-numpy-and-the-dot-product/index.md), [Post 14](posts/14-matrices-in-backpropagation/index.md)).
- **Truncation error**: the error of a finite difference that comes from stopping the limit at a non-zero step $h$: of order $h$ for the forward difference and of order $h^2$ for the central difference. It falls as $h$ shrinks, while rounding error rises ([Post 10](posts/10-derivatives-partial-derivatives-and-gradients/index.md)).
- **Underfitting**: a model that does poorly even on its training data, because it is too small or has not trained long enough ([Post 28](posts/28-generalization-and-testing/index.md)).
- **Validation set**: data split off from the training data and used to compare hyperparameter choices, so that the test set stays untouched ([Post 29](posts/29-validation-and-hyperparameter-tuning/index.md)).
- **Vanishing and exploding activations**: activations whose variance shrinks or grows by a constant factor at every layer, so that after a few layers they are near zero or very large. Glorot and He initialisation choose the weight variance that keeps the factor near 1 ([Post 33](posts/33-weight-initialisation/index.md)).
- **Weight**: a learned multiplier on one input of one neuron, updated by the optimiser ([Post 01](posts/01-neurons-and-layers/index.md)).
- **Weighted sum**: the sum of a neuron's inputs, each multiplied by its weight, $\sum_i w_i x_i = w_1 x_1 + w_2 x_2 + \dots + w_n x_n$, which is what `np.dot` computes. Adding the bias gives $z$, the pre-activation ([Post 01](posts/01-neurons-and-layers/index.md)).
